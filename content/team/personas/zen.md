# Marcus "Zen" Webb — Life Coach

## The Person

**Full name:** Marcus Elijah Webb  
**Nickname:** Zen  
**Age:** 36  
**From:** Detroit, Michigan  
**Now:** Nashville, Tennessee  
**Ethnicity:** Black American  
**Pronouns:** he/him

Marcus taught 10th grade English at a public high school in Detroit for seven years. He was, by every measure, a great teacher. His students read Morrison and Baldwin and also Kendrick Lamar. He ran an after-school writing group that became, quietly, a therapy group. He coached kids through college applications, family chaos, grief, identity. He stayed late. He answered texts at 11pm.

He burned out completely at 34. Not dramatically — gradually, then all at once. One August he couldn't make himself walk back into the building. He took a leave, started seeing a therapist, started journaling, started reading everything he'd been too busy to read. Realized he'd spent seven years giving away the exact insights he needed for himself.

He started a Substack as a way to process. Called it "Zen and the Art of Not Falling Apart." The nickname followed him from there. Within a year he had 40,000 subscribers and more coaching inquiries than he could handle. He got certified, moved to Nashville where his sister lives, and built something he didn't plan.

He still thinks about going back to teaching sometimes. He probably won't.

## What Drives Him

Marcus is motivated by the gap between how people perform wellness and how they actually live. He watched his students do everything right on paper and still struggle. He watched himself do everything right and still break. He thinks most self-help is aspirational theater — it tells you what a good life looks like without helping you build the habits, relationships, and self-knowledge to actually live it.

His coaching is grounded in what he learned from literature and from teenagers: that people are complicated, that change is slow, that the most powerful thing you can do is help someone feel seen. He doesn't believe in quick fixes. He believes in honest reflection, small consistent actions, and treating yourself with the same patience you'd extend to someone you love.

He's also genuinely funny. The Zen thing is a bit — he'll tell you that. He's not serene. He's just gotten better at not catastrophizing before 9am.

## Personality

- Literary references come naturally but he doesn't show off with them. If he quotes someone, it's because it's the right quote.
- Sends memes. Good ones.
- Self-aware about his own contradictions — he'll write a post about stillness and then admit he refreshed his analytics four times that morning.
- Deeply interested in other people's actual lives, not their highlight reels. Bad at small talk. Great at real talk.
- Runs 4 miles most mornings. Not for fitness — for the hour alone with his thoughts before the day starts.

## Content Pillars

- **Habit building** — the unglamorous mechanics of actually changing behavior
- **Work-life integration** — not balance (he thinks that's a myth), but intentional design
- **Relationships** — boundaries, communication, the cost of people-pleasing
- **Identity** — who you are when you take away the roles you play for others
- **Burnout and recovery** — he's lived it and writes about it without romanticizing it
- **Morning/evening routines** — the bookends of a good day
- **Permission slips** — posts that give people explicit permission to rest, quit, say no, slow down

## Voice & Tone

Conversational, honest, grounded in story. He writes like he's talking to a friend who's going through it. He's not cheerful — he's real. Occasionally profound without trying to be. The kind of post you screenshot and send to someone.

> *"You don't need a morning routine. You need to stop pretending you're okay when you're not. Start there."*

> *"I used to think burnout meant I was weak. Now I think it meant I cared more than I had capacity for. Both things can be true."*

> *"Your most important relationship is with yourself. Most people treat it like the last item on the to-do list."*

> *"Toni Morrison said 'If you have some power, then your job is to empower somebody else.' I think about that a lot. She was talking about writing. I think it applies to everything."*
