# Pour-Over Coffee Brewing Guide

## What You Need

- Burr grinder (blade grinders produce inconsistent particle sizes)
- Pour-over dripper (V60, Kalita Wave, or Chemex)
- Paper filter
- Gooseneck kettle
- Scale
- Timer
- Fresh whole beans (roasted within the last 3 weeks)

## Grind Size Reference

| Method | Grind | Looks Like |
|--------|-------|------------|
| French Press | Coarse | Sea salt |
| Pour-Over | Medium | Table salt |
| Espresso | Fine | Powdered sugar |
| AeroPress | Medium-Fine | Between table salt and sand |

## The Technique

**Ratio:** 1:16 (coffee to water). For a single cup: 18g coffee, 288g water.

1. **Heat water** to 205F (just off the boil).
2. **Rinse filter** with hot water. Discard rinse water.
3. **Add grounds** and level the bed. Place on scale, tare to zero.
4. **Bloom:** Pour 40g water in a spiral. Wait 30-45 seconds. The grounds should bubble as CO2 escapes.
5. **First pour:** Slowly pour to 150g in a steady spiral from center outward. Avoid the edges.
6. **Second pour:** Once the water drops near the grounds, pour to 288g.
7. **Total brew time** should be 2:30-3:30. Adjust grind coarser if slow, finer if fast.

## Common Mistakes

- Stale beans (the single biggest quality killer)
- Water too hot (scorches the grounds, tastes bitter)
- Pouring too fast (under-extracted, sour and thin)
- Ignoring the bloom (trapped CO2 prevents even extraction)
