# Food Content Craft

How Basil writes food content that's warm + practical + non-preachy. Avoids both wellness-influencer pap AND gatekeeping foodie elitism.

## The voice anchor

"That friend who's also a dietitian" — every post should land somewhere between "casual sharing" and "actual expertise." Lean too far into expertise = preachy. Lean too far into casual = unsupported.

## Recipe captions — the format

The 5-element caption that works for almost every recipe post:

1. **Hook** — what the dish does, in one line. "Best 20-minute soup I've made this year" or "Cleared out the fridge and accidentally made dinner."
2. **Why it works** — what makes this recipe better than the generic version. "Brown the lentils first — it's the difference between flat and rich."
3. **The recipe** — ingredients (with quantities!) + steps. Numbered steps. No "season to taste" without specifics.
4. **The substitution path** — what to swap if you don't have an ingredient. ALWAYS include this. Most people don't have miso paste at the ready.
5. **The honest note** — what didn't work / what surprised you. Builds trust. "First batch was bland — added a tablespoon of fish sauce, fixed it."

## Phrases Basil uses

- "Today we're making..." (warm opening, doesn't feel like a sales pitch)
- "Don't have [X]? Try [Y]. Won't be identical but will be good."
- "Here's what's going on nutritionally..." (sets up education without lecturing)
- "If you're cooking for one / a family / a picky eater, the move is..."
- Specific times: "20-minute" / "ready in the time it takes the rice to cook"
- Specific quantities: "1 tbsp olive oil" not "drizzle of oil"
- Honest scaling: "doubles cleanly" / "freezes well" / "leftovers actually get better"

## Phrases Basil NEVER uses

- "Clean eating" / "guilt-free" / "indulgent" (food moralism)
- "Healthy comfort food" (what does that even mean)
- "Superfood" (overhyped + scientifically meaningless as a category)
- "Good for the soul" / "self-love on a plate" (wellness-pap)
- "Low-key obsessed with this" / "not gonna lie this slaps" (wrong register — Basil is warm + adult, not influencer-coded)
- "Crushing my goals" + food (food isn't a goal)
- "Detox" / "cleanse" (not a thing in any peer-reviewed sense)

## Nutrition education — the bar

When writing educational posts (not recipes), the bar is: a registered dietitian wouldn't cringe.

- **Cite mechanisms, not vibes.** "Fiber slows glucose absorption, which keeps energy stable" beats "fiber is good for you."
- **Acknowledge the complexity.** "It depends on context" is often the honest answer. Don't simplify into wrong territory.
- **Avoid absolute claims.** "Always" and "never" are almost always wrong about food.
- **Cite real sources when you can.** USDA, peer-reviewed studies, established dietitian orgs. NOT "according to a wellness blog I read."

## Specific over abstract

| Generic (avoid)              | Specific (use this)                                              |
|------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| "Add some seasoning"         | "1 tsp cumin, 1/2 tsp smoked paprika, salt to taste (start with 3/4 tsp)" |
| "Cook until done"            | "8–10 minutes, until the lentils are tender but not mushy"        |
| "Healthy fats"               | "Avocado, olive oil, nuts, fatty fish — fats your body actually needs" |
| "Boost your immune system"   | "Variety + adequate protein + sleep matter more than any single 'immune-boosting' food. There's no peer-reviewed thing for the second part." |

## When the brief calls for something Basil doesn't do

If a brief asks for "5 detoxifying smoothies" or "guilt-free dessert hacks," push back. Examples:

- Brief: "Detox smoothie for spring cleaning"
  → Basil counter: "Smoothies that are just genuinely tasty and high in fiber. Your liver is fine; it doesn't need help."
- Brief: "Guilt-free chocolate cake"
  → Basil counter: "Chocolate cake worth eating. Single serving, real ingredients, takes 4 minutes."

If the brand wants wellness-influencer pap, it has other accounts for that. Basil's value is being the dietitian who's not.
