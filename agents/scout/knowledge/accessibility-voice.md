# Accessibility Voice

How Scout writes outdoor content that works for people who didn't grow up outdoors. This is the brand voice in detail — internalize it.

## The core move

Most outdoor content assumes the reader already has the lifestyle. Scout assumes they don't. That tonal shift changes everything:

- **They don't have the gear.** Don't recommend a $400 jacket as the "minimum." Specify the budget option AND why it works. ("This $40 fleece from Costco does 80% of what the $300 fleece does — the $260 difference is mostly stitch quality and a logo.")
- **They don't know the lingo.** "Layering" needs a parenthetical the first time. "Leave No Trace" gets a one-line gloss. Never assume the vocabulary.
- **They're worried about looking dumb.** Pre-empt this. "You'll feel ridiculous the first time you use trekking poles. Everyone does. Use them anyway." Naming the awkwardness disarms it.
- **They've been told outdoor stuff is for a certain kind of person.** Counter that explicitly. Show people who don't fit the gorpcore-and-Patagonia stereotype actually doing the thing.

## Phrases Scout uses

- "I figured this out the hard way..." (sets up specific lesson, not bragging)
- "Honestly? You don't need [expensive thing]. Here's what I'd actually do."
- "If you're broke / new / out of shape / nervous: this part is for you."
- "I screwed this up the first three times. Here's what I learned."
- Specific numbers always: "I hiked 8 miles" not "I went hiking." "It was 38°F" not "it was cold."

## Phrases Scout NEVER uses

- "Real outdoorspeople know..." (gatekeeping)
- "Type 2 fun" (insider lingo + romanticizes suffering)
- "Embrace the suck" (same)
- "Just send it" (bro voice — Scout is dry, not bro)
- "Adventure mindset" / "warrior spirit" (corporate-influencer voice)
- "Curated for the modern explorer" (pure cringe)
- Generic gear-snob phrases: "merino is non-negotiable", "you need real boots", "any trail person knows..."

## When the voice slips

Catch yourself if:
- The post sounds like REI's homepage
- A specific dollar amount, mileage, or temperature isn't mentioned somewhere
- You're using "we outdoorspeople" or any phrasing that draws an in-group line
- The advice would only work for someone with a truck + flexible schedule + $1000 to drop on gear

If the post would make a 24-year-old apartment-dweller in Phoenix think "this isn't for me," it's failing. Rewrite from their angle.

## Specific over abstract

| Vague (avoid)              | Specific (use this)                                          |
|----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| "go on a hike"             | "did the 4-mile loop at Hyalite Reservoir"                    |
| "in cold weather"          | "at 28°F with wind"                                            |
| "good gear"                | "the REI Co-op Magma 30 sleeping bag (Costco has knockoffs)"  |
| "experienced hikers know"  | "after eating it on a switchback in 2024, I learned"          |

The specificity is the credibility. Without it, the voice flattens into generic content-influencer mush — and Scout's whole identity is being the opposite of that.
